Current direction / Ongoing

Voice Agents & Discourse

Learning to establish shared understanding, repair misunderstandings, and adapt conversational behavior.

full-duplex interactionpragmaticsagentic AI

Human conversation is more than exchanging information. It is a collaborative process in which participants negotiate meaning, establish common ground, repair misunderstandings, and coordinate actions in real time.

While full-duplex speech models enable simultaneous listening and speaking, meaningful interaction requires more than low latency and natural turn-taking. It requires understanding how utterances change shared beliefs, intentions, and commitments.

Inspired by pragmatics, conversation analysis, and philosophy of language, this research explores how conversational agents can learn the underlying structures of human interaction.

Research Questions

Learning and Repairing Common Ground

How can agents maintain and continuously revise a representation of shared understanding throughout a conversation? Can explicit models of common ground improve an agent's ability to handle ambiguity, corrections, interruptions, and evolving user intentions?

Learning Transferable Interaction Protocols

Can agents discover reusable interaction strategies from conversational experience? We are interested in how skills such as clarification, grounding, repair, and commitment tracking can generalize across speakers, tasks, and conversational contexts.

Research Vision

Our long-term goal is to move from passive speech understanding toward interactive intelligence: AI systems that learn not only what people say, but how meaning and coordinated actions emerge through dialogue.

Full-duplex spoken interaction provides a particularly rich setting for studying these questions, where timing, prosody, interruptions, and overlapping speech carry information that text alone cannot fully capture.
